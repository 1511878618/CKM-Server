Overview

Why CKM Score?

Cardiovascular, kidney, and metabolic diseases share deep pathophysiologic connections. The CKM syndrome defines this interrelationship, and CKM Score provides a quantitative framework to assess the risk of progression to its most severe stage.

Cardiovascular Risk

Integrates established cardiovascular risk estimates (PREVENT-CVD) and atrial fibrillation genetic risk (CHARGE-AF) to capture cardiovascular susceptibility.

Metabolic & Kidney

Encompasses metabolic dysfunction and kidney disease burden through the PREVENT-CVD composite, which includes CKD and metabolic components.

Genetic Predisposition

Incorporates the CKM Polygenic Risk Score (PRS), capturing inherited susceptibility to cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic disease progression.

About the Model

A New Integrated Assessment Paradigm

The CKM Score model predicts the risk of progression from CKM Stage 0-3 to CKM Stage 4 — the clinical endpoint defined by the AHA as overt cardiovascular disease with metabolic risk factors and/or kidney disease.

Unlike traditional single-system risk scores, CKM Score combines genetic predisposition (CHARGE-AF, CKM PRS), a validated cardiovascular risk equation (PREVENT-CVD), and clinical variables (age, sex) into a unified logistic regression framework.

Variable Definitions

Model Input Variables

The CKM Score model integrates five variables spanning genetic risk, clinical risk scores, and demographics. Each variable is described below.

CHARGE-AF Genetic Risk Score
Description

The CHARGE-AF (Cohorts for Heart and Aging Research in Genomic Epidemiology - Atrial Fibrillation) genetic risk score quantifies an individual's inherited susceptibility to atrial fibrillation based on a weighted sum of genetic variants (SNPs) associated with AF risk.

Input Format

Expressed as a percentage (0–100%), representing the estimated absolute risk of atrial fibrillation.

Source

Derived from genome-wide genotyping data using the CHARGE-AF polygenic risk score weights. Requires genetic testing or imputed genotype data.

Key Reference

Lubitz SA, et al. Circulation. 2014;129(16):e311-e312.

PREVENT-CVD Clinical Risk Score
Description

The PREVENT (Predicting Risk of cardiovascular disease EVENTs) CVD risk score, developed by the American Heart Association, estimates the 10-year risk of total cardiovascular disease (including heart failure, atrial fibrillation, and coronary heart disease). It incorporates age, sex, blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes, smoking, eGFR, UACR, and BMI.

Input Format

Expressed as a percentage (0–100%), representing the estimated 10-year absolute CVD risk.

Source

Calculated using the AHA PREVENT risk equations. Can be obtained from the AHA PREVENT Calculator.

Key Reference

Khan SS, et al. Circulation. 2024;149(20):e871-e886.

Age Demographic
Description

Patient age at baseline assessment, in years. Age is a fundamental demographic variable in cardiovascular risk modeling and contributes to CKM progression risk assessment.

Input Format

Integer value, 18–100 years.

Source

Self-reported or medical record age at time of assessment.

Sex Demographic
Description

Biological sex as recorded in the model. In the CKM Score equation, male sex is coded as 1 and female sex as 0, reflecting sex-based differences in cardiovascular and metabolic disease risk trajectories.

Input Format

Binary: Male = 1, Female = 0.

Source

Biological sex as recorded in the medical record.

CKM PRS Polygenic Risk Score
Description

The CKM Polygenic Risk Score (PRS) is a composite genetic score capturing inherited susceptibility to cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic disease progression. It aggregates the effects of multiple genetic variants (SNPs) associated with CKM-related pathways, including cardiovascular, renal, and metabolic traits.

Input Format

Continuous score, typically distributed around 1.0. Values above 1.0 indicate above-average genetic predisposition; below 1.0 indicates below-average risk.

Source

Derived from genome-wide genotyping or imputed genotype data using CKM PRS weights. Requires genetic testing or biobank data.

CKM Stages

CKM Syndrome Clinical Stages

Per AHA definition, CKM syndrome spans Stages 0-4. This model predicts progression from Stages 0-3 to Stage 4.

0

At Risk

CKM risk factors present (e.g., obesity, hypertension) but no metabolic abnormalities or subclinical target organ damage.

1

Metabolic Dysfunction

Glucose metabolism abnormalities (e.g., prediabetes) or moderate-high risk CKM factors, with or without kidney impairment.

2

Subclinical Organ Damage

Subclinical cardiovascular disease or CKD progression (e.g., decreased eGFR, elevated UACR) with persistent CKM risk factors.

3

Subclinical CVD

Subclinical atherosclerosis or high-risk cardiovascular findings, with metabolic abnormalities and/or kidney disease.

4

Clinical CVD Endpoint

Clinical cardiovascular disease (e.g., MI, heart failure, stroke) with metabolic abnormalities and/or kidney disease. This is the predicted outcome.

Risk Equation

The CKM Score uses a multivariable logistic regression model to estimate the probability of CKM Stage 4 progression.

P(CKM Stage 4) = 1 1 + e−Z

Risk Stratification Thresholds

The CKM Score classifies patients into four risk categories based on the predicted probability of progression to CKM Stage 4. Thresholds are aligned with the PREVENT-CVD risk stratification framework and will be refined in collaboration with clinical partners.

< 5%
Low Risk

Low probability of CKM Stage 4 progression. Maintain healthy lifestyle and periodic screening.

5% – <20%
Moderate Risk

Moderate risk. Address modifiable factors and schedule regular follow-up.

20% – <40%
High Risk

Significantly elevated risk. Prompt clinical evaluation and targeted intervention advised.

≥ 40%
Very High Risk

Substantially elevated risk. Comprehensive intervention strongly recommended.

Development & Validation

Model Development and Validation

The CKM Score was developed and externally validated using large-scale population-based biobank cohorts.

Development Cohort

UK Biobank (UKB)

The CKM Score model was developed in the UK Biobank, one of the world's largest prospective population-based cohorts, comprising approximately 500,000 participants aged 40-69 years with extensive genetic, clinical, and longitudinal follow-up data.

Cohort UK Biobank
Role Model Development
Design Prospective Cohort
External Validation

All of Us (AoU)

The model was externally validated in the All of Us Research Program (AoU), a landmark national precision medicine initiative by the U.S. National Institutes of Health, enrolling over one million diverse participants with rich genomic and electronic health record data.

Cohort AoU
Role External Validation
Design Prospective Cohort
External Validation

Mass General Brigham Biobank (MGBB)

The model was further validated in the Mass General Brigham Biobank (MGBB), an academic medical center-linked biobank with linked genomic and longitudinal electronic health record data from one of the largest hospital-based research cohorts in the United States.

Cohort MGBB
Role External Validation
Design Hospital-based Cohort
Detailed model performance metrics (C-index, calibration, sensitivity/specificity) will be added upon publication. The model is currently under peer review.
